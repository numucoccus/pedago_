### Final Round — Problem Statement

## AI for Academic Life

University faculty members have to deal with a wide range of responsibilities beyond teaching in the classroom. Consider a faculty member at AUST who is asked to introduce a new course. They may need to design a curriculum and syllabus that fits within the university's academic standards while also ensuring that it complements existing courses. The same faculty member may later need to prepare an examination. They might wonder whether the questions adequately cover the course, whether they are sufficiently diverse, whether similar questions have already appeared in previous years, or whether the assessment fairly measures what students were expected to learn.

When it comes to grading, the challenges continue. Multiple faculty members may evaluate the same examination, potentially leading to differences in interpretation and grading. Establishing a clear and consistent standard for evaluation can itself be a difficult task.

And these are only a few examples.

From designing courses and preparing assessments to evaluating students and improving academic processes, faculty members encounter countless problems that require time, expertise, and judgment.

## The Challenge

How can AI make the academic lives of university faculty better?

Your task is to identify one or more meaningful problems faced by faculty members and build an AI-powered solution to address them.

You are not required to solve any particular problem.

You may focus on curriculum design, assessment, grading, course management, academic quality, research-related work, administrative tasks, or a completely different problem that you believe is important.

The problem you choose to solve and the way you choose to solve it, are entirely up to you. We encourage participants to think beyond simply using AI to generate content. Consider how AI can help faculty understand, reason, discover, compare, evaluate, automate, or make better decisions.

The goal is not to replace the faculty member. The goal is to give them a better tool to do their work.

## Think Beyond the Obvious

There is no single correct solution.

One team might build a system that helps faculty design better courses. Another might tackle assessment quality. Someone else might identify an entirely different problem that nobody else considered.

The most valuable solution may be the one we didn't think to mention.

## Think in Terms of One Useful Journey

You do not need to build an entire academic management platform.

A strong MVP may solve just one small problem well:

**Faculty gives something → your system intelligently processes it → faculty receives something useful.**

For example, a system could inspect, compare, organize, check, recommend, highlight, evaluate, or improve something. What that “something” is remains entirely your choice.

## Make It Demonstrable

By the end, the judges should be able to see:

**the problem → the input → what the AI does → the useful result.**

A smaller idea with a convincing working flow is better than a large idea where most features exist only in the presentation.

## Going Further

Once your core workflow works, you may extend it with features such as explanations, visualizations, history, feedback, document handling, collaboration, automation, personalization, or anything else that makes your solution more useful.

These are possibilities, not requirements.

**Find a real problem. Build the smallest version that genuinely helps. Then make it yours.**
